01

An oil shock is turning into a borrowing-cost shock

The war in West Asia started as an energy story. It is now changing what money costs, from Washington to small-cap India.

The fighting that began in February, when the US and Israel struck Iran, has moved to the Red Sea. Attacks hit the Saudi Aramco East-West pipeline, which carries crude across Saudi Arabia to the port of Yanbu. On 15 September Saudi Arabia stopped loading oil at Yanbu, a port that can ship up to 5 million barrels a day. Brent crude, the main world oil price, moved towards $110 a barrel, and the US price went above $105, up 57% since 2 July.

Diesel is where the pain is sharpest. US diesel hit a record $6.26 a gallon, up over 80% in 9 months, and on world markets it traded above $200 a barrel. Diesel moves trucks, ships, farm machines and mines, so a diesel spike reaches the price of almost everything, which is why central banks are reacting.

The US Federal Reserve, led by Kevin Warsh, was priced at a 93% chance of raising its rate, its first hike since July 2023. That is the twist: President Trump appointed Warsh expecting cuts. The European Central Bank has already raised rates for the 2nd time this year. Long-term borrowing costs jumped too: the UK 30-year gilt (a British government bond) hit 5.95%, its highest since 1998, and Japanese yields hit a 30-year high.

India felt it through leverage, meaning money borrowed to buy shares. On 15 September over 900 Indian stocks fell 3% or more, the first such day since May. A microcap index fell about 7% in under 24 hours, from 26,800 to 25,100. Brokers issued margin calls (demands for more cash when loans against shares go bad), so owners sold even their steady shares to cover the losses in risky ones. The Nifty 50 touched a 5-month low.

02

Physical limits that money cannot fix fast

Refining, memory chips, missiles and grid approvals are all short at once, and each one takes years to build more of.

Diesel is short because of refining, not only crude

Crude oil must be refined before a truck can burn it. The gap between the price of diesel and the price of crude, called the crack spread, hit a record. Russia, the 2nd-biggest diesel exporter, has already stopped exports and is weighing a longer ban. The US, the biggest exporter, is now discussing its own ban; Senate leader John Thune said he is open to it. Barclays warned such a ban could backfire, because other countries would then bid even harder for the fuel.

US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said the government may use the Defense Production Act, an emergency law, to add refining capacity.

The US emergency stock is thin too. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve, the crude the government keeps for a crisis, fell another 403,000 barrels to 285 million, its lowest since 1982. Flow through the Strait of Hormuz, the channel between Iran and Oman that carries much of the Gulf's oil, is averaging 11 million barrels a day. Saudi Aramco is trying to restore half of the East-West pipeline within days after 3 pumping stations were hit, and oil prices eased on that report.

Memory chips are the other squeeze

Tim Cook of Apple called memory pricing a "100-year flood". Apple raised prices by $100 on 4 iPhones already on sale with the same hardware, so the 2TB iPhone 18 Pro Max now costs $500 more than the model it replaced. Cheap phones are hit harder, so in India phones under ₹10,000 are disappearing (see section 07).

The US: a weak auction and a Fed boxed in

When the US Treasury sells bonds, it wants the price to land close to what the market expected. For the 20-year bond it missed by 2 basis points (0.02 of a percentage point), the biggest miss since December 2024, and the 5.42% yield is the highest since the bond was brought back in 2020. Foreign buyers took only 52.5%.

Goldman Sachs and Rabobank argue the Fed does not need to hike; JPMorgan expects the S&P 500 to fall 1.25% to 1.75% if it does not. Since 2022 the US 10-year yield has averaged 4.17%, double the 2.06% of the 10 years before.

2 things did not follow the textbook. Higher US yields usually lift the dollar, but the dollar index has not risen. And gold usually rises in a war, but it fell as rates rose, because gold pays no interest. Chinese gold funds still added 11 tonnes in August, reaching 293 tonnes.

India: $136 billion arrived, and it is not all free

The RBI ran a dollar swap window that drew $136.37 billion; $127 billion of that came through FCNR(B), foreign-currency deposits from Indians abroad. Both figures are being quoted as "the FCNR number", but they measure different things.

The inflow pushed deposit growth to 17.76%, while credit grew 19.08%. Sanjay Agarwal of CARE Ratings says the jump from about 14% to 18% is "only because of FCNR". So the RBI is now draining cash through reverse repo auctions (where banks park spare money with the RBI) and ₹1 lakh crore of bond sales.

The risk is timing. These are dollar deposits that must be repaid. If the rupee stays weak while foreign investors keep selling, which they have done for 5 straight days in the cash market, the cash cushion can become a bill. Measured in dollars, the Nifty 50 is back where it was 5 years ago.

04

Exports jumped, but read the base and the price before cheering

India's August exports rose 26%, the war reshaped shipping routes, and rebuilding Saudi oil sites may send orders to Indian pipe makers.

India's goods exports rose 26.1% in August to $43.81 billion, while imports rose 14.1% to $70.67 billion. The trade deficit (imports minus exports) narrowed to $26.86 billion, a 5-month low. It was the 5th straight month of double-digit export growth, and services exports added an estimated $38.87 billion.

3 things flatter the headline. August last year was weak, with exports down 9.5%, so the base is low. Petroleum products rose mostly because fuel prices rose. Electronics imports also jumped 40% to $13.65 billion, so much of the phone export is assembly of bought-in parts.

Still, 68 of 168 product groups grew in both value and physical volume, so part of the growth is real extra output, not just higher prices. Gold imports fell 58% to $2.3 billion after a duty hike, which also helped the deficit.

Rice shows how the war moves goods

Rice, India's biggest farm export, grew only 2.17% to $4.81 billion in April to August. Ports in Iraq, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Dammam and Jebel Ali are disrupted, Jeddah has faced Houthi attacks, and ships now route through Sohar and Muscat in Oman and Khor Fakkan in the UAE. Gulf buyers are purchasing only what they need now, because freight is dear.

Who could get paid for rebuilding

If Saudi oil and gas sites need rebuilding, pipe and project companies could get priority orders. The names being linked to this are Man Industries, Maharashtra Seamless, Welspun Corp, Ratnamani Metals & Tubes, VA Tech Wabag for water, Kalpataru Projects International and Larsen & Toubro. This is an idea, not an order book yet, and 2 of them, Welspun Corp and VA Tech Wabag, fell about 8% to 10% in the 15 September sell-off.

High crude helps producers. Antelopus Selan Energy has risen on oil prices, and its bigger bet is the Duarmara field in Assam with Oilmax Energy, linked to Asian Energy Services, guided at 6,200 barrels a day at peak. First output is due in FY27, after 1 delay.

05

UPI gets a price tag, and the market sold the news

From 15 October some shops will pay a fee on large UPI payments. Customers still pay nothing, but the rule reshuffles who earns what.

For the first time since 2020, NPCI, which runs UPI, will charge a Merchant Discount Rate (MDR). An MDR is a small cut of each payment that a shop pays to the banks and apps that carry it. The rule: 0.4% on shop payments above ₹2,000, capped at ₹300 once a payment reaches ₹75,000. Person-to-person transfers stay free, and so do shops that receive under ₹1 lakh a month through UPI QR codes.

The rule hits few payments but a lot of money. About 96% of shop payments are under ₹2,000, yet the ones above ₹2,000 carry about 67% of shop payment value. In August UPI handled 24.51 billion payments worth ₹29.82 lakh crore, an average of about ₹1,216 each.

A widely shared breakdown of a ₹50,000 payment got the arithmetic wrong. It split ₹150 among the banks and apps and added ₹27 of GST. But 0.4% of ₹50,000 is ₹200, and 18% GST on that is ₹36, so the shop pays ₹236 and keeps ₹49,764. So the true cost to a shop is 47.2 basis points (0.472%) once GST is added, and some shops will try to pass it on.

How the market priced it

The likely winners earn this fee: Paytm, Pine Labs, Yes Bank (partner bank for PhonePe), Axis Bank (for Google Pay), State Bank of India, ICICI Bank and HDFC Bank. Morgan Stanley sees Paytm's profit estimates for FY28 and FY29 rising 38% to 48%.

Yet Paytm and MobiKwik fell after the news, because Paytm had already risen 71% in 6 months on this hope. Cash-handling firms CMS Info Systems and Radiant Cash Management were seen as winners if some shops return to cash; PPFAS Mutual Fund bought 20 lakh CMS shares, taking its stake to 9.83%.

Anger is loud. A petition in the Supreme Court challenges the fee, and the government has refused to back down. The Finance Ministry denied that foreign pressure shaped the rule.

Other rule changes

  • EPFO: the wage ceiling for compulsory provident fund rises from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000 a month, bringing over 51 lakh more workers in, so take-home pay dips and savings rise.
  • Exports: shipments up to ₹3 lakh no longer need an RCMC (an exporter registration certificate), which helps first-time sellers.
  • Gold: the hallmarking fee rose 67%, from ₹45 to ₹75 per item.
  • Foreign investors: registration now takes 5 days instead of 30 or more.
  • Roads: the NHAI barred PNC Infratech from new bids for 3 years after damage on the Kanpur-Lucknow Expressway.
06

Where the value sits: good businesses at flat prices, and forced listings

Insurers grew while their shares stood still, Tata Sons is being pushed to list, and defence know-how is moving to private firms.

Insurance: the business grew, the price did not

ICICI Prudential Life shares cost about the same as in 2019, while its embedded value (an actuary's estimate of what its policies are worth to shareholders) rose about 2.5 times. HDFC Life shares are lower, and its parent HDFC Bank, which put in ₹1,000 crore at ₹688.52 a share in June, is already down 24%.

The reason is rules, not results. Since 2023 almost every change moved money from shareholders to policy buyers. GST on individual policies went to zero in September 2025, but insurers lost the tax credit on their own costs, which cost HDFC Life about 3 percentage points of margin. From 1 January 2027 banks cannot force a policy on a loan customer.

Owners are changing too. Prudential plc must cut its ICICI Prudential stake from 21.9% to under 10%. Foreign funds cut HDFC Life from 25.6% to 19.3% while Indian mutual funds raised theirs from 10.6% to 16.6%, so these shares are moving from foreign money to SIP money.

The next test is the IRDAI paper on commissions. Reports say it sets only a direction, with no rates, from around April 2027, which helps PB Fintech; deeper cuts for bank sellers would hurt Max Financial Services and HDFC Life.

PB Fintech kept ₹1,888 crore of revenue from ₹8,372 crore of premium in Q1 FY27, and adjusted EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and write-offs) more than doubled to ₹186 crore. Its engine is renewals, earned at about 80% margin and growing 55%.

Tata Sons must face a market price

On 11 September the RBI rejected Tata Sons' request to give up its core investment company licence, which would have let it stay unlisted. It then filed a caveat in the Bombay High Court to block any surprise stay.

The big winner is Shapoorji Pallonji Group, which owns about 18.4% of Tata Sons, worth ₹2.5–3 lakh crore. It carries ₹55,000–60,000 crore of debt, partly refinanced at 18.95%, so a market price gives its lenders a clear value to lend against.

Defence know-how moves to private firms

At the VIMARSH meet, DRDO handed 9 technology licences to 13 companies. Bharat Dynamics and Apollo Micro Systems will build a naval mine; Ananth Technologies and Apollo will make laser seekers; Premier Explosives and Solar Defence & Aerospace will make missile propellants. Mazagon Dock Shipbuilders plans a ₹27,000 crore shipyard at Dighi, Raigad.

07

Indian spending is splitting: the top trades up, the bottom borrows

Cheap phones are vanishing, car numbers look strong on a weak base, and more families are pledging gold to borrow.

The ₹10,000 phone is disappearing

Phone prices in India have nearly tripled in under 10 years, and as memory got dear, big brands left the cheap end. IDC data shows phones under ₹10,000 fell 74.3% in Q2 2026, and their share of the market shrank from 15.6% to 4.5%. Yet that market is still 40–50 million buyers, far bigger than the roughly 30 million who buy high-end phones.

So smaller brands like Itel and Lava are bringing back phones with only 2GB or 3GB of RAM at ₹8,000–9,000. People who want an iPhone buy a used one: refurbished phone sales grew 13% in H1 2026, and Apple is 65.3% of Cashify's refurbished sales.

A clear shift: CNG, electric and hybrid cars together now outsell petrol's 40.8% share for the first time, as high fuel and loan costs push buyers to cheaper running costs. Gold is becoming a loan

Indians have borrowed over ₹9 lakh crore against gold, and gold loans from NBFCs (non-bank lenders) rose 68.5% in a year. High gold prices let families borrow more, and so much borrowing shows many households need cash.

The wider pulse

GST collections grew 14.8% in August, but domestic GST grew only 9.3%; import-related tax did the rest. The manufacturing PMI (a monthly survey of factory activity where above 50 means growth) fell to 52.8, a 5-year low but still growth. Retail inflation stayed above the RBI's 4% target for a 3rd month. Unemployment eased to 5.0%, and job postings on Naukri rose 14%, with AI jobs up 31%.

At the top, State Bank of India found 1.7 million customers with ₹2.4 lakh crore to invest. In mass brands, Dabur's profit grew from ₹1,448 crore in 2020 to ₹1,947 crore, slower than inflation.

08

Where the story has run ahead of the numbers

AI valuations, IPO pricing and small-company order wins all need a second look at what has actually been earned.

AI: huge spending, thin proof so far

OpenAI is in early talks to raise money at over $1.2 trillion, up about 41% from $852 billion in March. Its Q2 revenue grew 18% to $6.7 billion, but its operating margin fell. Bank of America notes that $1.5 trillion spent on AI in 3 years has shown little proof of economy-wide productivity gains so far. On the other side, Thomas Kurian of Google Cloud says AI servers pay back in under 2 years, and in half that on Google's own chips, with most contracts locked for 5 years.

Both can be true: the sellers of compute are earning, while the users of AI have not yet shown it in national data.

The data-centre debate has 2 camps as well. Headlines say local bans are killing US building. SemiAnalysis mapped over 300 local moratoriums (temporary bans): about 20 gigawatts of planned capacity sits inside restricted areas, but only about 1.5 gigawatts is actually delayed. It forecasts 18 GW of new US capacity in 2026 and 38 GW in 2027. Brad Gerstner of Altimeter Capital expects only 25 GW next year and calls the AI trade "fully valued".

09

China builds, America argues, Europe pays more to borrow

The same technologies are being handled very differently, and India is picking partners in the gaps.

In China, electric and hybrid cars now take 70.3% of retail car sales in September so far, while total car sales fell 23% from a year ago. EV sales fell only 10%, so most of the drop is in petrol and diesel cars. BYD will spend about $100 million on a battery-storage factory in Brazil, because Brazil's grid-battery tender demands local production; CATL and Jinko have made similar plans. China's tech firms are also borrowing for AI: ByteDance raised a $29.6 billion unsecured loan from about 30 banks.

That is new for China, where lending is normally tied to land and buildings, not to computers that age in 3 years.

In the US, AI has become an election issue. Dario Amodei of Anthropic has called for pacing, meaning slowing the most powerful models until safety catches up. Mark Zuckerberg of Meta answered that risks from new science are best met by speeding up, though Meta did delay its Muse agent by months for safety. Jensen Huang of Nvidia said firms should not release products they doubt, but no new laws are needed.

In Europe, French finance minister Roland Lescure called the slow-down talk a ploy by US labs to stay ahead, and a proposal says Europe needs 20 GW of data centres by 2028 and 100 GW by 2030.

India's openings

Japan is expected to give 10–15% of India's space-tech business; Bellatrix Aerospace already makes parts for 2 Japanese firms. In batteries, the cathode is 42% of a cell's cost; Gujarat Fluorochemicals and Himadri Speciality Chemical lead Indian work, but for most players this is a story for FY29 and beyond.

10

Arguments worth keeping open

Where careful people disagree, both sides are laid out with a way to check who is right.

Should UPI stay free for shops?

The case against the fee, made loudly by Ashneer Grover, is that UPI is public infrastructure that saves India money elsewhere. Handling cash through ATMs and cash vans is estimated to cost about ₹30,500 crore a year, so a fee that pushes even some shops back to cash could cost the country more than it raises. The government has also already paid about ₹8,700 crore in incentives across FY22 to FY25 to keep UPI free, which shows the system was never free to run.

The case for the fee, made by the RBI and NPCI, is that UPI now handles 680–760 million payments a day, bank servers strain at festival peaks, and someone must pay for security and fraud checks. Vishwas Patel of the Payments Council of India says the fee will fund the system rather than create profit pools.

Former RBI deputy governor R. Gandhi suggested a digital rupee (CBDC) could let shops avoid the fee on large payments. The fair test comes after 15 October: if shop payments above ₹2,000 keep growing at the same pace, the friction argument weakens; if they fall while cash withdrawals rise, it wins.
